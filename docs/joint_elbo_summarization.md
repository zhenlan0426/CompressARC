# Joint ELBO for Candidate Selection in ARC-AGI

This document summarizes a brainstorming session on refining the ARC-AGI model's approach to predicting output Y given history H (input-output examples). The goal is to make the process more principled by incorporating a joint probabilistic perspective while leveraging the existing VAE-like framework.

## Original Approach in Codebase
- **Step 1**: Optimize latent parameters μ (for z) and decoder f to maximize log P(H) via ELBO: E_z [log P(H | z, f)] - KL(q(z; μ) || p(z)).
- **Step 2**: For prediction, compute argmax_Y log P(Y | μ, f) using the optimized model.
- **Key Files**: ELBO computed in `take_step` (train.py/train_batch.py), candidate selection in solution_selection.py.
- **Pros**: Efficient; fits model to observed data first.
- **Cons**: Ad-hoc; doesn't directly optimize for the joint P(Y, H).

## Proposed Refinement: Joint ELBO for Scoring Candidates
- Generate candidate Y_i using the current process (e.g., sampling/argmax from output logits after fitting to H).
- Score each Y_i using an ELBO approximation of log P(H, Y_i): Reuse ELBO(H) terms and add E_z [log P(Y_i | z, f)] (reconstruction term for Y_i, similar to H's cross-entropy + grid marginalization).
- Select the Y_i with the highest joint ELBO score.

### Original Probabilistic Framing
The refinement is inspired by a more principled approach: Start from P(Y|H) and assume a latent model where the joint P(Y,H,z;μ) = P(Y|z) * P(H|z). Then P(Y, H) = sum_z P(Y,H,z;μ), maximized over μ, and P(Y|H) = P(Y,H) / P(H) (ignoring the constant denominator for argmax over Y). This directly targets the posterior P(Y|H) by maximizing the joint P(Y,H) over μ (and summing over z), then argmax over Y—since P(H) is constant.

- **Rationale**: Approximates max_Y P(Y | H) ∝ max_Y P(Y, H) by ranking candidates under the joint, assuming conditional independence P(H, Y | z) ≈ P(H | z) * P(Y | z).
- **Integration**: Track output logits during training (via Logger in solution_selection.py). Evaluate Y_i in a post-processing loop, reusing masks and marginalization logic from take_step.

## Strengths
- **Principled Yet Practical**: Bridges ad-hoc fitting with Bayesian joint maximization without full re-optimization.
- **Low Overhead**: Reuses forward passes; only adds lightweight scoring per candidate.
- **Handles Uncertainty**: Easily incorporates grid size marginalization (e.g., logsumexp over sizes/offsets).

## Weaknesses and Inconsistencies
- **ELBO Looseness**: As a lower bound, it may not perfectly rank candidates if the variational approximation varies.
- **Independence Assumption**: May undervalue correlations between H and Y given z.
- **Candidate Bias**: Limited to Y_i generated by the H-fitted model; misses out-of-distribution true Y.

## Potential Pitfalls
- **Compute Cost**: Scoring many candidates could slow down for large grids or batches.
- **Overfitting/Noise**: KL term might favor "simple" Y_i; unstable logits during training could lead to inconsistent scores.
- **Grid Handling**: Ensure consistent mask usage (uncertain vs. perfect) between training and evaluation.

## Suggestions for Implementation
- **Hybrid Scoring**: Combine joint ELBO with original log P(Y_i | μ, f) for robustness.
- **Diverse Candidates**: Use temperature sampling or perturbations to expand the candidate set.
- **Prototype**: Modify solution_selection.py to add ELBO scoring function. Test on simple tasks with known metrics (e.g., via plot_accuracy.py).
- **Extensions**: Fine-tune z/f lightly on top candidates for better joint fit.

This approach could improve prediction accuracy for ambiguous tasks while staying efficient. Next steps: Prototype in code and evaluate on benchmarks.
